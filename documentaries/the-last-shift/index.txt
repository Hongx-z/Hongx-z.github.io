10:T5b1,<p>Nineteen years is a long time to stand next to a machine. Long enough that the machine becomes a kind of colleague — not a friend, exactly, but something you notice the absence of.</p>
<p>This film covers the last eight hours of the plant's operation. It is deliberately small. There is one location, one principal subject and no narration, because everything I wanted to say was already being said by the paperwork Fatima had to fill in.</p>
<h2 id="directors-note"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#directors-note">Director's note</a></h2>
<p>I went in expecting to make a film about loss, and I had to throw that film away on the second night. What was actually happening was more complicated and much better: a party with a job in the middle of it.</p>
<p>The film is built around ninety seconds at 03:40, when the compressed-air line was bled down — forty years of pressure leaving a building through a valve the size of your wrist. Everyone stopped and listened. Nobody said anything.</p>
<p>Everything else in the eighteen minutes exists to make that moment land. If you watch it and the bleed-down feels quiet rather than loud, the film has done its job.</p>
