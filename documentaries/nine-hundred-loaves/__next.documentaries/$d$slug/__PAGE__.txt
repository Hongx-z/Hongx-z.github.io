3:T534,<p>There is a version of this film that is about tradition, and there is a version that is about labour, and I tried hard to make the second one.</p>
<p>Auntie Fola starts at 02:15. By 06:00 she has produced nine hundred loaves, six hundred and forty of them a white tin loaf she has described to me, twice, as "the boring one". She is sixty-three. She has done this six days a week, with three interruptions for surgery, for twenty-six years.</p>
<h2 id="directors-note"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#directors-note">Director's note</a></h2>
<p>The whole film takes place in one room, and for the first four minutes you cannot see very much, because neither can she — the lights stay off until the dough has been divided.</p>
<p>I made this film because of a number. Nine hundred is not a remarkable figure for a bakery; industrial bakeries do far more. What interested me was that she knows the number exactly, every day, without counting, and can tell you at any point in the shift how many are left to do. That is a kind of internal arithmetic that runs constantly in the background of a person's mind, and I wanted to film somebody who has it.</p>
