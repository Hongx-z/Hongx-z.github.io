Nineteen years is a long time to stand next to a machine. Long enough that the machine becomes a kind of colleague — not a friend, exactly, but something you notice the absence of.

This film covers the last eight hours of the plant's operation. It is deliberately small. There is one location, one principal subject and no narration, because everything I wanted to say was already being said by the paperwork Fatima had to fill in.

Director's note

I went in expecting to make a film about loss, and I had to throw that film away on the second night. What was actually happening was more complicated and much better: a party with a job in the middle of it.

The film is built around ninety seconds at 03:40, when the compressed-air line was bled down — forty years of pressure leaving a building through a valve the size of your wrist. Everyone stopped and listened. Nobody said anything.

Everything else in the eighteen minutes exists to make that moment land. If you watch it and the bleed-down feels quiet rather than loud, the film has done its job.

A note on participation

Fatima saw the cut before anyone else did. She asked for one change — not a removal, a correction: I had mis-stated the year she joined. She was right.
