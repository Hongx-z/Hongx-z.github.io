10:T926,<p>I have spent about ten years writing about work, and the last four of those also filming it. The through-line is narrow and slightly obsessive: <strong>what people actually do all day</strong>, described without romance and without condescension.</p>
<h2 id="what-i-do"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-i-do">What I do</a></h2>
<p>I write long-form reportage and essays, and I make short observational documentaries. The two feed each other. Reporting teaches you where to stand; filming teaches you when to shut up.</p>
<p>Most of my subjects come from the same source: somebody mentions a job I did not know existed, and I cannot let it go. A water reader who can tell you the level of an aquifer by the sound of a pump. A woman who has baked the same nine hundred loaves every morning for twenty-six years.</p>
<h2 id="how-i-work"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#how-i-work">How I work</a></h2>
<p>Two rules, arrived at slowly and defended stubbornly.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Two visits before the camera comes out.</strong> The first visit is for talking. The second is for sitting quietly and being boring, which is when people stop performing. Only then does the equipment come out of the bag.</li>
<li><strong>The subject reads the cut before anyone else does.</strong> Not to approve it — I keep final cut — but so that nobody is surprised on the day it goes out. This has cost me a scene twice, and both times the film was better for it.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p>I am not interested in the version of a person that exists for an audience. I am interested in the version that exists at 4 a.m. on a Tuesday, which is a much harder thing to get at.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="a-note-on-this-site"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#a-note-on-this-site">A note on this site</a></h2>
<p>This site is deliberately split in two, because the two halves of the work are not the same thing.</p>
<p><strong>About me</strong> is the written record: a timeline of what I have done, the honors that came with it, and every article I have published. <strong>About others</strong> is the video record: short films about specific people, each one under half an hour, each one about somebody who is not me.</p>
