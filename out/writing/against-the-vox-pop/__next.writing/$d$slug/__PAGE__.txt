3:T1082,<p>The vox pop is a format in which a reporter stands in a public place and asks strangers a question about the news, then broadcasts the forty most confident seconds out of the ninety minutes of footage they collected. I have made dozens of these. I would like to explain why they are almost always a lie.</p>
<h2 id="the-format-is-a-claim-not-a-survey"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-format-is-a-claim-not-a-survey">The format is a claim, not a survey</a></h2>
<p>A vox pop makes an implicit argument: <em>here is what people think.</em> It is dressed as evidence. It is presented with the visual grammar of evidence — ordinary people, ordinary street, unpolished delivery. And it is not evidence of anything, because the selection is entirely aesthetic.</p>
<p>The people who appear in a vox pop are the people who appear well. They are fluent, they are not in a hurry, they are not holding a child, they do not have a bus to catch and they are not, in the ordinary statistical sense, representative of the street they are standing on. We know this. We select for it deliberately, and then we broadcast it as though we had not.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Ask a hundred people a question and eighty of them will say "I don't really know enough about it." That is the honest answer. It is never the answer that makes the cut.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="what-the-format-does-to-the-person-in-it"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-the-format-does-to-the-person-in-it">What the format does to the person in it</a></h2>
<p>Something specific and slightly ugly happens. A person who has been handed a microphone for the first time in their life almost always tries to give you the answer they think a reasonable person would give. They perform reasonableness. They perform the middle. This is not stupidity — it is politeness, and it is a completely rational response to being filmed by a stranger.</p>
<p>Which means the vox pop systematically extracts <strong>the performance of consensus</strong> and then reports it as consensus itself. The format does not merely fail to measure public opinion. It manufactures a version of it, out of the least interesting thing in any given person: their desire to seem sensible.</p>
<h2 id="the-people-who-do-it-well"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-people-who-do-it-well">The people who do it well</a></h2>
<p>There is a version of this I admire, and it is worth naming because it is the opposite of what I have been describing.</p>
<p>The good version asks a <strong>specific</strong> question to which the person has a real answer, by virtue of being who they are at that moment. Not "what do you think about the hospital closure" but "how far is it to the nearest one from here". Not "are you worried about inflation" but "what did this cost last year".</p>
<ul>
<li>Specific questions get specific answers, which are informative.</li>
<li>Specific answers reveal the shape of the actual thing.</li>
<li>A hundred specific answers add up to something you did not know when you started.</li>
</ul>
<p>The general question, by contrast, gets the general answer, which is always roughly the same in every country on earth and tells you nothing about any of them.</p>
<h2 id="where-that-leaves-me"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#where-that-leaves-me">Where that leaves me</a></h2>
<p>I still make them, because they are cheap and they fill ninety seconds and a producer is asking. But I have started writing the question on paper and reading it aloud to myself before taking the camera out, and asking one thing: <em>could a person give me a real answer to this, or only an opinion about their own personality?</em></p>
<p>If it is the second one, I do not shoot it. It is not much of a rule. It has killed maybe a fifth of what I would otherwise have filmed, and every one of those deaths was correct.</p>
<hr>
<p>The last vox pop I made, I asked the question, got eleven answers, and used none of them. I used the twelfth: a man who said he did not know, and then talked for two minutes about why he did not know, and in doing so described the entire problem better than the nine hundred words I filed around him. He got nine seconds. He should have got the whole piece.</p>8:X
