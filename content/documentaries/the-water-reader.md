The first thing Idris said to me was that he was not a scientist. The second thing he said was that the scientists call him twice a year.

He walks a route of eleven pumps and listens to each one for between thirty seconds and two minutes. From that he produces a number — the depth of the water table, to within about a hand's width. The catchment authority has a logger at four of the eleven sites. He has been right about the other seven, and about the four, more often than the loggers have.

## Director's note

This is a film about a skill that is about to stop existing, and I wanted to avoid the two obvious traps: making it a lament, and making it a mystery.

So the film does not ask *how does he know*. It asks what his day is like, and how long the walk is, and what he does when he is wrong — which happened once during the shoot, and which is the reason this film exists in the form it does.

The answer to the question nobody asked: he says he can hear the difference between water at eleven metres and water at twelve. He cannot explain it further. Neither can the two hydrologists I showed the footage to, who both said, independently, that they believed him.
